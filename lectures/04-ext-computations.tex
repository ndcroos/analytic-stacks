Today I want to talk about some $\Ext$ computations in (light) condensed abelian groups.

The basic claim is that this category of condensed light abelian groups is a very convenient framework for doing homological algebra. It is a very nice abelian category. You can form a derived category without any problem. Because topological spaces essentially embed faithfully into condensed sets, topological abelian groups also basically embed faithfully (under some mild assumptions).

Now you have a nice home to play with them, but because you have an abelian category, you somehow have all $\Ext$ groups, and then you can wonder what they are. For this theory to be somewhat useful, you want all the answers of these $\Ext$ computations to give you reasonable answers that are interpretable.

% 2:04
The first theorem that I stated last time is the following theorem. One thing you can do is start with, for example, some nice geometric object like a CW complex $X$. Let's say $M$ is an abelian group. Then one can look at the $\Ext$ groups in light condensed abelian groups, between the free condensed abelian group on $X$ (or rather, on the corresponding condensed set), and $M$ treated as a discrete abelian group.

\begin{theorem}
Let $X$ be a CW compex, and $M$ an abelian group. \\
Then 
\begin{align*}
\Ext^i_{\Cond\Ab^\light} (\Z [\underline{X}], M) \simeq H^i_\sing(X, M)
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}
